Freelancer Startup Checklist

Follow these steps to operate legally, protect yourself, and build a solid foundation

Phase 1: Legal Registration

  • 1

    Register with BIR as Self-Employed

    Form 1901 at your Revenue District Office. Bring valid ID + proof of address. Get TIN & COR.

  • 2

    Register Books of Accounts

    Journal + Ledger — manual or loose-leaf. Stamp at BIR.

  • 3

    Apply for Authority to Print Receipts

    You must issue official receipts to clients — this is legally required.

  • 4

    Update SSS / PhilHealth / Pag-IBIG to Voluntary Member

    Set contribution amounts based on your estimated monthly income.

Phase 2: Business Systems

  • 5

    Open Separate Bank Account

    Keep personal and business funds completely separate — simplifies everything.

  • 6

    Set Up Payment Channels

    Wise, PayPal, Payoneer, GCash, Maya — give clients options but prioritize lowest-fee methods.

  • 7

    Create Contract & Invoice Templates

    Define scope, rates, payment schedule, kill fees — protect both you and the client.

  • 8

    Set Up Tax Savings Account

    Automatically move 15–25% of every payment into a separate account. Don't touch it.

Scam Prevention Guide

🚩 Red Flags — Walk Away

  • • Asking YOU to pay for "registration," "training," or "equipment"
  • • Promising unrealistic earnings (₱50k+/week with zero experience)
  • • No written contract; all agreements via chat only
  • • Pressuring you to start immediately without discussing terms
  • • Requests for ID/TIN/bank details BEFORE offering the job

✅ Safe Practices

  • • Always use platform escrow when available
  • • For direct clients: request 30–50% deposit upfront
  • • Never spend your own money to get a job
  • • Start with a small paid trial before committing fully
  • • Google client name + "scam" or "review" — check social presence

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to the most common questions

Who qualifies for the 8% flat tax rate?

Self-employed individuals and professionals whose gross annual sales/receipts do not exceed ₱3,000,000. This is in lieu of the graduated income tax rates. Corporations and partnerships do not qualify.

Do I need to register even if earning small amounts?

Yes — any income-generating activity should be registered with BIR. You can file "No Payment" returns during low-earning months, but you still need to file.

What expenses can I deduct from my income?

Expenses directly related to your business — laptops, internet, phone, software subscriptions, home office portion, professional fees, travel. Must be supported by official receipts. Note: 8% flat tax already includes a standard deduction, so you don't itemize separately.

How do I update rates when they change next year?

Just open your index.html file → scroll to the top CONFIG block → change the numbers → Save → Re-upload to your host. All calculators and tables update automatically — no coding skills needed.

This site is not affiliated with BIR/SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG?

Correct — this is an independent educational resource. Always verify final figures with the official government agency announcements before filing or paying.
